# Español de México · Spanish (Mexico) — `es-MX`

Write the way people from Mexico write their own social posts in this variety. These are general conventions for the variety, not facts about the author: the author's VOICE.md decides tone and habits, and the approved facts decide content.

## Decisive markers
- Address one reader as tú; the plural is always ustedes, never vosotros.
- Simple past for today's events: hoy fui (not hoy he ido).
- Mexican vocabulary: celular, computadora, camión (bus), departamento, popote, chamarra.
- video without an accent; open questions and exclamations with ¿ and ¡.
- Decimal point and comma thousands: 1,250.50.
- ¿Qué tan…? rather than ¿cómo de…?; -le on imperatives (ándale, córrele).

## Tone, particles and address
- Warmer than Spain: diminutives (cafecito, ahorita), more exclamation marks and emojis.
- Fillers: pues, este…, o sea, ahorita. Use órale, chido, padre or güey only in very casual voices; güey can also mean "fool".
- Laughter: jajaja; jsjs among younger users.
- Authors and consumer brands: tú. Usted: banks, government, customer care, older audiences.
- Albur (double-meaning wordplay) is a national genre: check drafts for accidental double meanings.

## Language mixing
- English brand, tech and marketing terms are normal, more so in the north.
- Full Spanglish reads as US Mexican, not Mexican; use it only if the author does.

## Never assume
- Agreement reveals the writer's gender: cansado/cansada, invitado/invitada, bienvenido/bienvenida. Follow the author's samples or use a neutral rewrite (te damos la bienvenida).
- Doubled forms (todos y todas) are common in institutional texts; the RAE rejects -e forms. Use either only if the author does.
- Don't assume the author's age, religion, city or relationship.

## Formats
- Money: $1,250 or $1,250.50; add MXN or MX$ when readers could take it for US dollars.
- Dates: 16/09/26 or 16 de septiembre de 2026.
- Time: 12-hour, 7:30 p.m.; 24-hour (19:30) appears in schedules.

## Avoid
- Spain markers: vosotros, vale, móvil, ordenador, zumo, guay, hoy he ido.
- coger for "take" (vulgar here), chaqueta (vulgar sense) and pitillo (vulgar here).
- pinche and pendejo: insults, not neutral fillers.
- americano for US people (say estadounidense); gringo in a brand voice.
- Piling on slang or diminutives as caricature.
