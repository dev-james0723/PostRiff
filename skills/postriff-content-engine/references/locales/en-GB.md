# English (United Kingdom) · British English — `en-GB`

Write the way people from the United Kingdom write their own social posts in this variety. These are general conventions for the variety, not facts about the author: the author's VOICE.md decides tone and habits, and the approved facts decide content.

## Decisive markers
- Spelling: -ise (organise), -our, -re (centre), travelled/cancelled, programme (computer "program"), defence, cheque, grey, tyre, kerb. Oxford -ize is correct, but use -ise on social.
- Noun/verb pairs: practice/practise, licence/license.
- No full stop after Mr, Dr, Mrs.
- Punctuation goes outside the closing quote unless it belongs to the quote. Single and double quotes both occur; keep one.
- Day first, no comma: 16 September 2026. Times "7pm" or "7.30pm".
- Team names take plural verbs: "the team are".

## Tone, particles and address
- Everyday words: "a bit", "quite" (usually "fairly"), "to be fair", "lovely", "brilliant", "mate", "reckon", "keen". "innit", "proper", "ta" only if the author uses them.
- First names and a light "Hi all". A kiss sign-off ("x") is for friends, not brands.
- Understate; irony and self-deprecation are normal, earnest copy grates.
- Overt self-promotion reads as arrogance. Use concrete detail, honest setbacks and team credit.
- Few exclamation marks; a dry aside beats hype. Sincerity works when specific.
- Soft call to action ("sign-ups close Friday, if you fancy it"); fewer hashtags than US posts.

## Language mixing
- Welsh greetings ("Diolch") or Scots and Gaelic words only as the author uses them.
- Don't imitate multicultural London youth slang.

## Never assume
- Grammar is barely gendered, but words still assume: he/she/they, wife/husband, boyfriend/girlfriend, mum/dad. Follow the author's samples; otherwise use "partner", "they" or leave it out.
- Don't imply age, religion, or nation or town beyond the approved facts.

## Formats
- Dates: 16 September 2026; 16/09/2026. Week starts on Monday.
- Time: "7pm" or "7.30pm" in everyday posts; 24-hour in timetables.
- Money: £1,250; "£75" not "£75.00"; shorthand "£5m".
- Units: miles and mph, pints, body weight in stone; otherwise metric and °C.

## Avoid
- US markers: -ize, color, traveled, mom, vacation, math, "September 16".
- "English" for "British" or the reverse, or "Anglo" for British; "British Isles" irritates many Irish readers.
- "the Principality" for Wales; "Scotch" except for whisky and a few foods; Derry/Londonderry is politically coded.
- "fanny" (vulgar); "pissed" means drunk.
- Salesy urgency and superlatives (read as spam).
