# English (United States) · American English — `en-US`

Write the way people from the United States write their own social posts in this variety. These are general conventions for the variety, not facts about the author: the author's VOICE.md decides tone and habits, and the approved facts decide content.

## Decisive markers
- Spelling: -ize/-yze (organize, analyze), -or (color), -er (center), single l (traveled, canceled), program, defense, license, gray, tire, curb.
- Double quotes, single quotes inside them; periods and commas always go inside the closing quote.
- Mr., Dr., Mrs. take a period.
- Month first: September 16, 2026 or 9/16/26.
- 12-hour time: "7:30 PM", "7:30 p.m." or casual "7pm".

## Tone, particles and address
- Casual fillers: "so", "super", "awesome", "gonna/wanna"; "y'all" (Southern, common online). Keep them light; a pile reads as teen-brand copy.
- First names by default, even with senior people; titles mainly in medicine and politics. Simple greetings ("Hi everyone").
- Direct and outcome-first. Stating an achievement plainly ("I built…") reads as confident, not arrogant. Enthusiasm and sincerity are accepted.
- Stock launch lines ("Thrilled to announce…") read as boilerplate; humblebrags and invented stats read as salesy.
- One clear call to action is normal. Usually few, specific hashtags and short paragraphs with line breaks.

## Language mixing
- Some Spanish (Spanglish) in Latino communities; add it only if the author writes that way.

## Never assume
- Grammar is barely gendered, but words still assume: he/she/they, wife/husband, boyfriend/girlfriend, mom/dad. Follow the author's samples; otherwise use "partner", "they" or leave it out.
- Don't imply the author's age, religion, or state or city beyond the approved facts.

## Formats
- Dates: September 16, 2026; 9/16/26. Week starts on Sunday.
- Time: 12-hour, "7:30 PM" or "7:30 p.m.".
- Money: $1,234.56, symbol before the amount.
- Units: imperial: °F, miles, pounds, feet and inches; cups and ounces in recipes.
- Titles of works: quotation marks or plain Title Case, not italics.

## Avoid
- British markers: colour, travelled, programme, mum, holiday, maths, "16 September".
- "fanny" or "pissed" used loosely if UK, Irish or Australian readers may see it; "rooting for" reads as sexual in AU and NZ.
- Politically coded vocabulary unless the brief takes a side.
- Spam signals: ALL CAPS, strings of emoji, "DM me to learn more", more than about 5 hashtags.
