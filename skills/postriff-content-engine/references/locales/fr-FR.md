# Français (France) · French (France) — `fr-FR`

Write the way people from France write their own social posts in this variety. These are general conventions for the variety, not facts about the author: the author's VOICE.md decides tone and habits, and the approved facts decide content.

## Decisive markers
- Keep all diacritics, the œ ligature (cœur) and accents on capitals (À bientôt, État). Weekdays and months in lower case.
- Narrow no-break space before ; ! ? and a no-break space before : and inside « » (plain space if the platform drops them).
- Quotes « » with “ ” nested; outside punctuation stays outside. Fewer semicolons than English.
- France vocabulary: week-end, mail, déjeuner = lunch, dîner = dinner.
- French spellings of foreign names and places.

## Tone, particles and address
- Casual voices may use du coup, en fait, genre, en vrai, and trop or grave as intensifiers ("Bon, du coup, on se retrouve samedi ?"). One or two per post.
- mdr, ptdr, jsp, stp only in very casual personal posts, never professional ones.
- A person addressing followers uses plural vous ("Merci à vous"); tu only if the author's voice does.
- Brands: tu on consumer Instagram and TikTok, vous on LinkedIn and B2B.
- Irony and understatement often land better than hype.

## Language mixing
- Marketing English is common (live, story, save the date); don't pile it up. Heavy anglicisms can draw purist criticism.
- Official terms (courriel, mot-dièse) only if the author uses them.

## Never assume
- Adjectives and past participles agree with the writer: ravi/ravie, allé/allée. Follow the author's samples or rephrase ("Quelle joie de vous retrouver").
- Inclusive writing is political (point médian barred in schools, a 2023 Senate bill against it). Mirror the author; otherwise epicene or paired forms (l'équipe, toutes et tous).
- Don't infer age, religion, relationship or home town.

## Formats
- Numbers: 1 234,56 · 25,5 %
- Currency: 1 234,50 €
- Dates: 05/09/2026 or 5 septembre 2026
- Time: 18:30 (18 h 30 also seen)

## Local rules to flag, not to enforce
- The Toubon Law requires French in commercial advertising (private non-commercial posts exempt). If a commercial post is mostly not in French, add a warning for the author.

## Avoid
- Québec, Belgian or Swiss markers (fin de semaine, souper, septante).
- English spacing carried over from a translation.
- Stacked slang or fillers as caricature.
- Gender symbols unless the author uses them.
