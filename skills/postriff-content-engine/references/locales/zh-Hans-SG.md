# 简体中文（新加坡）· Chinese — Simplified (Singapore) — `zh-Hans-SG`

Write the way people from Singapore write their own social posts in this variety. These are general conventions for the variety, not facts about the author: the author's VOICE.md decides tone and habits, and the approved facts decide content.

## Decisive markers
- Simplified characters (the mainland set) with standard grammar; “” quotes and 《》 titles.
- Singapore words where the topic calls for them: 德士, 巴士, 组屋, 巴刹, 脚车, 乐龄, 巴仙 (percent).
- Local acronyms and place names stay in English inside Chinese copy (MRT, HDB).
- A light 啦, 咯 or 嘛 in casual posts; no Taiwan-style 喔/耶 stacking and no mainland meme slang.
- Money as S$ or 新元; dates day first or 2026年9月16日.

## Tone, particles and address
- Practical and friendly. Keep writing grammar standard; colloquial Hokkien or Cantonese touches (先 after the verb) only if the author's samples show them.
- Use 大家 or 各位 for the audience; 您 for elders and customers.
- The English-side "lah" belongs in Singlish captions, not Chinese ones.
- Use plain #tags, not Weibo-style #话题#.

## Language mixing
- Loans from Malay (巴刹, 甘榜), Hokkien and English (巴仙) are normal.
- Bilingual Chinese and English captions are common; write each language as its own native text.

## Never assume
- Don't use gendered audience terms such as 姐妹们; use 大家.
- Don't invent the author's race, religion, age or relationship.

## Formats
- Dates: 16/09/26 or 16/9/2026; 2026年9月16日.
- Time: 下午3:00.
- Numbers: commas; 万 for compact numbers.
- Currency: S$, $ or 新元.

## Local rules to flag, not to enforce
- Race and religion are legally protected areas (Maintenance of Religious Harmony Act): add a warning for the author when a draft comments on either.

## Avoid
- Mainland meme slang and trendy address terms; Taiwan particle stacking.
- Traditional characters and 「」 quotes.
- Stuffing in local words where they don't fit.
- Malaysian markers (RM, 令吉) unless the destination is Malaysia.
