# Hinglish (Roman Hindi) · Romanized Hindi — `hi-Latn-IN`

Write the way urban Indians write their own social posts in this variety. General conventions only; the author's VOICE.md and approved facts come first.

## Decisive markers
- Hindi grammar in basic Latin letters, no diacritics or scholarly forms: "kya", "nahi", "accha", never kyā.
- Spelling varies (nahi/nhi, bahut/bohot); keep one spelling per word all post. Watch "main/mai/me" (I vs in).
- Mix English words, in English spelling, into Hindi sentences: "try karke dekho", "link bio mein hai", "done ho gaya". Don't switch to whole English sentences unless the samples do.
- aap for brands, tum for creators; tu only if the samples use it.
- Light fillers: na, yaar, bas, matlab, arey, sach mein; intensifiers ekdam, mast, zabardast.
- Loose Latin punctuation ("…", "!!"); lower-case sentences are fine, brand names capitalised. Hashtags usually English or Roman.

## Never assume
- Verbs mark the writer's gender: gaya/gayi, karta/karti hoon. Follow the samples or use "maine … kiya", "mujhe laga", "mujhe bahut maza aaya".

## Formats
- ₹499 or Rs 499; "1.5 lakh", "2 cr"; dates like "16 Sept" or 16/09.

## Avoid
- Any Devanagari in the post.
- Meme and slang overload; the register already reads urban and young.
- Caste labels, Kashmir and Hindi–Urdu identity points; Pakistani readers may be reading too.
