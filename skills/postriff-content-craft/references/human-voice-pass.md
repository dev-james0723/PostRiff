# Human voice pass

Use this pass on every draft after the meaning, evidence and platform shape are
in place. It adapts the useful parts of the `humanizer` method to the voice this
workspace has approved. The creator's supplied wording, approved examples and the
Content Engine remain the authority.

## Anchor the voice

Anchor to this creator's approved voice (`VOICE.md`) and supplied material, not
to a house style: there is no default personality to impose. Prefer wording and
rhythm already present in the creator's own writing. Preserve their register,
any natural code-switching between languages or dialects, uncertainty, humour,
asides and unusual phrasing when they are theirs. A formal voice may stay formal.

Do not add opinions, memories, feelings, slang, jokes, typos or verbal tics to
make copy seem human. If a personal reaction matters and the creator has not supplied
it, ask one focused question or leave the reaction unresolved.

## Remove synthetic patterns

Read the draft aloud and revise clusters of these patterns when they make the
copy sound generated:

- inflated claims about importance, transformation or the future;
- promotional superlatives, vague praise and generic inspirational endings;
- corporate filler, abstract nouns and elaborate wording where a direct verb is
  clearer;
- vague attribution, unsupported certainty and invented connective analysis;
- mechanical groups of three, synonym cycling and evenly shaped sentences;
- repeated contrast formulas, stacked fragments and manufactured punchlines;
- tutorial signposting, fake-candid rhetorical openers and chatbot phrases;
- repeated em dashes, decorative formatting, emoji labels or forced hashtags.

Use concrete sourced details, simple constructions and varied sentence lengths.
Variation should follow the thought rather than becoming a new formula. Detect
patterns in context instead of banning an ordinary word or punctuation mark.

## Preserve meaning and personality

Humanization is an editorial pass, not a factual or personality rewrite. Keep the
source claim, qualification, attribution and the creator's actual point of view intact.
A precise sentence may stay polished. A short sentence may stay short. An em dash
or a repeated phrase may remain when it is intentional and sounds like the creator.

Do not optimize for detector scores or claim that text is undetectable. The goal
is natural authorship that the creator can recognize, defend and approve.

## Final check

Before returning the copy, confirm:

1. The creator could reasonably say every first-person claim from the supplied context.
2. The opening earns the body and the ending says something specific.
3. The copy sounds natural when read aloud and does not fall into a repeated
   template rhythm.
4. The edit preserved evidence, nuance, platform fit and the creator's voice.
5. Paragraph-based public copy has exactly one blank line between paragraphs.

Keep this audit out of the public copy. Mention only material voice choices or
unresolved personal context in the craft note or PostRiff warnings.
