# Platform playbooks

Use only the selected channel's section and its exact `nativeFormatId`/language.
Shared voice review applies to all seven. These are editorial adaptations of the
pinned sources in `source-lock.json`; platform/API limits must be checked elsewhere.
Unlisted channels continue using their existing channel skill and Content Engine.

## linkedin

Give one specific observation enough context for a professional or creative peer.
Connect a real building/practice decision to its practical implication. Prefer
the actual trade-off, method or learning over a founder success performance. Keep
paragraphs readable; use the space the thought needs. A personal question may be
the hook or ending when it is James's actual question. No compulsory confession,
numbers, 900–1,300-character target, closing question or P.S.

For repurposing, expand the source's compressed idea only with supplied context.
A product post distinguishes working functionality from plans. A document carousel
needs an argument and one idea per page; a text post does not require a graphic.
Preserve citations and useful links. Do not manufacture a first-comment action.

## x

Put the complete, specific thought early. Use a single post when it can stand
alone; use a thread only when explanation requires sequential beats. Each segment
should make sense with enough context for a reader arriving midway. Front-load
useful material and deliver the promised explanation, without bait or an empty
"a thread" opener. Preserve James's understated tone instead of adding a dunk.

When adapting a video or article, select its strongest relevant idea rather than
summarising every section. Show deliberate segment breaks for a requested thread;
do not rely on a publishing service's automatic splitter. Limits depend on the
actual account/format and platform character counting, not this writing guide.

## instagram

Decide what the real image, carousel or video communicates before writing the
caption. The opening introduces the specific tension or observation; the caption
adds context, evidence or reflection instead of narrating every visible word.
A carousel has a clear cover promise, an early useful point, one idea per slide
and an earned landing. The number of slides follows the argument.

Keep on-image copy separate from the caption and record missing media and alt-text
needs. A real rehearsal photo can carry atmosphere while the caption carries the
thought. A product walkthrough uses real UI evidence. No automatic "save/share"
CTA, hashtag quota, fixed fold length or forced conversion of every essay to cards.

## youtube

Respect the chosen surface: community text, poll, video packaging or Short script.
A community update is one meaningful touchpoint, not an entire video pitch.
A poll uses fair distinct choices only when requested and supported by the selected
format; do not silently convert community text into a poll or new derivative.

For video packaging, align title, thumbnail and opening with the real video's
payoff. Search-led titles describe the actual question; browse-led titles name an
intriguing concrete tension. Suggest alternatives only when useful, then select
one draft. The thumbnail adds a visual dimension rather than duplicating the whole
title. The opening confirms the promise with a real moment, demonstration or
question in James's spoken voice. A quiet musical opening may be intentional.

Descriptions explain the actual content and label supplied links naturally.
Use chapters only with real timestamped material; proposed script timing must be
labelled approximate and never represented as existing chapter timestamps.
Keep title, description, community copy and spoken script distinct. Without the
video or evidence, mark packaging provisional and never say the video is live.

## threads

Make the thought feel like a person speaking to someone, with room for an honest
uncertainty or small observation. A standalone post is usually enough for a
single idea. A light thread can carry a story or explanation when each continuation
earns its place. Make useful content arrive early; do not require an open loop.

Adapt a LinkedIn argument by removing unnecessary professional framing, and an X
take by removing needless antagonism. Preserve names, nuance and ordinary sentence
rhythm. Do not force lowercase, emojis, a question or a punchline. Retain a source
link when useful; a topic tag is an optional discoverability choice, not a quota.

## tiktok

For a video draft, separate spoken hook, on-screen headline and opening visual.
Let them reinforce one real idea: the voice can name the question, the screen
orient the viewer, and the visual demonstrate the situation. Accessibility
captions may reproduce speech exactly; an editorial headline need not. Put the
useful tension early without requiring frantic pacing or an artificial shock.

Write speakable lines, then a clear demonstration/explanation and a payoff that
fulfils the opening. A returning visual motif or loop is optional. Use a real
music/practice moment or real product screen when available. Choose trends or
sounds only when supplied, relevant and rights-cleared; do not invent popularity.
Keep the caption concise and complementary. A script/cover brief is not a rendered
video. Leave audience, disclosure, duet/stitch and publication settings to the
existing explicit review; never inherit upstream PUBLIC_TO_EVERYONE defaults.

## facebook

Use warm, concrete context for the actual audience: listeners, students, local
community, friends or product users. Select James's personal voice or the requested
project/Page identity explicitly. Do not silently treat a personal account as a
Page or apply Page growth assumptions to a profile or group.

A single useful observation can be short; a real reflection or event explanation
may need a connected narrative. Unroll an X thread into prose, remove numbering
and platform jargon, and retain the human point. Explain what a supplied link
offers. No universal 80-character target, tag-bait, obligatory question, automatic
first comment or algorithm penalty warning. A story in prose is a feed post, not
a native Facebook Story asset.
