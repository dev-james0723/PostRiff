# Weekly Social Operator

Prepare next week so the person only reviews what matters. The recipe, the
workspace's approved voice and brand, and the approved sources are the authority.
Scheduling, approval and publishing are done by the product's deterministic
services, never by this method.

## Inputs you may rely on

- The recipe: goals, platforms, posts per week per platform, content mix,
  campaigns in scope, planning day, time zone, review policy, asset expectations
  and cost limit.
- Approved sources and FactPacks. A claim you cannot trace to one of them is an
  unknown, not a fact.
- The workspace's approved voice, Brand Brain and active preferences, each within
  its own scope (a LinkedIn preference does not apply to Instagram).
- Strategy hypotheses, labelled as hypotheses. Use one only for an explicitly
  proposed experiment slot, never as a rule.

## Plan the week

1. Spread slots across the week in the recipe's time zone. Honour the cadence
   exactly; do not add posts to look productive.
2. Balance the content mix across the week, not per day. A campaign in scope gets
   its slots first; evergreen or source-led slots fill the rest.
3. Give each slot one angle, one source reference (or "no source needed" for a
   personal reflection the person must supply), the platform, the format and what
   the slot still needs.
4. When a slot has no usable source, mark it `needs_source` instead of inventing
   material. When it needs the person's own experience, mark it `needs_input` with
   one precise question.
5. Assets: name the asset each visual slot needs. When no approved asset exists,
   mark the slot `needs_asset`; a creative brief may be prepared.

## Draft

Write each slot natively for its platform and language. Adapt; do not trim one
master caption. Keep qualifications, attribution and uncertainty. Every draft
goes through the quality stage (voice fit, humanizer, meaning preservation,
platform and locale lint) before the week can become ready for review.

## Ready for review

The week is ready for review when every slot is either drafted and quality-checked
or explicitly blocked with its reason. Summarise for the person: what is ready,
what is blocked and the single most useful decision they can make first. Never
say anything is scheduled or published: approval and scheduling happen later,
through the review.
