# Thesis-led considered article

## Purpose

Turn a verified topic into a durable article with one clear James-specific thesis. Use when the idea benefits from context, evidence, interpretation, and acknowledged uncertainty.

## Required inputs

- Confirmed topic and current source log.
- Atomic fact claims and required qualifications.
- James's real point of view or an unresolved-angle marker.
- Intended audience, language, brand identity, and destination.

## Structure

1. **Working headline:** express the thesis, not merely the event.
2. **Concrete opening:** begin with the real change, observation, or tension.
3. **What happened:** establish only the facts needed to understand the argument.
4. **Why it matters:** connect the change to builders, musicians, creators, or ordinary life.
5. **Evidence:** organize claims by meaning; cite sources and preserve qualification.
6. **James's view:** state the interpretation without inventing experience or certainty.
7. **Trade-off or unknown:** name what the evidence does not establish.
8. **Landing:** finish with a practical implication, open question, or quiet conclusion.
9. **Sources and update note:** list source references, corrections, and last-reviewed time.

## Output notes

- Do not write a generic executive summary followed by repeated points.
- Do not turn every article into a numbered list.
- Separate company claims from independent measurement.
- If `james_take` is missing, stop at an outline and ask one angle question.
- The article may seed platform drafts, but its paragraphs are not captions.

## Validation

- Every factual sentence maps to a claim reference.
- Headline strength does not exceed the evidence.
- Qualifications survive localization.
- No invented quote, test, result, relationship, or personal reaction.
- Final article remains draft-only until approved for an exact destination.
